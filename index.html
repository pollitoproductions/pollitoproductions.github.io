Welcome to Pollito Productions

Welcome to Pollito Productions — free Spanish games and learning tools that run right in your browser. No downloads, no accounts, no sign-ups. Just open a game or a tool and start learning.

I am Brant Fuller, a Spanish teacher, and I built these games for my own students first. Everything here is built around the same 1,000 Spanish words, with over 5,000 speaking and listening activities across the games and tools, so what you collect in a game is the same vocabulary you can practice in the tools, review in the lists, and print on the worksheets. Learn a word once, and you will keep meeting it everywhere.

Who is it for? Students learning Spanish, teachers looking for something students actually want to play, and anyone teaching themselves. The games are made for the classroom but fun enough for home. Play on the classroom board, hand out a worksheet for work away from the screen, or let students explore on their own devices. Nothing here needs an account, and your progress never leaves your browser.

What will you find? Four games. Vocabulario Español is the place to start if you’re brand new: a calm, open planet where you explore, find glowing vocabulary cards, and say each word aloud as you collect it. Backrooms: El Glosario Perdido is a spookier first-person hunt through endless yellow hallways for the lost pages of your glossary. Generation Ship is the biggest one: a 3D space station with real spin gravity, where you walk the decks, fly out to recover scattered words, and use nine learning-tool stations inside the ship itself. Spanish Dash is the newest: a fast runner where you jump spikes and platforms, and at every checkpoint you have to say the Spanish word out loud to keep going.

Around the games are the learning tools: flashcards and quizzes for the 1,000 words, cognates, verb conjugation, ser versus estar, everyday expressions, and dictation. The guides explain the grammar behind the words, and the resources page has free printable worksheets with a teacher guide and answer keys.

Where should you start? It depends on where you are. If you’re brand new to Spanish, start with the planet game — wander, collect words, and say them aloud. If you already know some words, open the vocabulary list to see and hear them all, then try the practice tool to see what stuck. From there, pick the learning tools that match your level — cognates and false friends, conjugation, ser vs. estar, saber vs. conocer, pronouns, por vs. para — or jump into the generation ship, which puts those same tools inside a 3D space station. That loop, play first and practice second, is how the whole site is meant to work.
